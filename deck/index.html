Last Saturday, the 3rd of October, a weather station in Barcelona recorded more than 190 millimetres of rain in a single day. That's about a third of a normal year's rain. Water like that is dangerous. Within minutes, streets became rivers, cars were swept away, and some people were trapped. A storm like this can take lives.

One found and checked all the data. One wrote the physics. One was an independent auditor that tested the maths and could stop us if something was wrong. But the flood map itself is not AI guessing. It's physics. We also built a machine learning model with XGBoost on a custom dataset; this tells us the margin of error. Based on this we know our ideas are validated. After the validation is done we allow the data to show up in the app.

Depth and speed together tell us whether a street is safe or dangerous.

We put a grid over Barcelona, like a chessboard with thousands of small squares. For every square, the computer calculates five things: how deep the water gets, how fast it moves, which direction it flows, when it arrives, and how long it stays. Because depth and speed together tell us if a street is safe or dangerous.
